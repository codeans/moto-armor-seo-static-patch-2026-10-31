Leather motorcycle jacket manufacturing

Moto Armor manufactures sport, café-racer and street leather motorcycle jackets for private-label brands and distributors. Jackets are cut in cowhide with armour pockets at the shoulders and elbows and an optional back-protector pocket.

You choose the leather finish, fit, venting, liner, hardware and branding on a paid sample. Samples take about 12–18 days and bulk about 30–45 days after approval. Minimums start from 50 units per style.

Jackets can be designed toward EN 17092 classes. A CE rating on an armour insert does not certify the whole garment; garment certification needs test reports for the exact build.

Leather Jackets FAQ

Can armour be specified as CE rated?

Yes. You can specify CE EN 1621-1 Level 1 or Level 2 armour inserts. That rating applies to the armour component, not automatically to the whole garment.

Do you provide size charts?

Yes. Each category can link a size chart. Custom programs can grade from your chart or a fitted sample.

How do I measure for a race suit?

Measure chest, waist, hip, inseam, sleeve and height in a riding posture. See our measuring guide for detail.

Can you grade a full size run from one sample?

Yes. Sample a mid size, approve fit, then we grade the bulk size breakdown you provide.

Which leathers do you offer?

Cowhide and kangaroo are common for race programs. Goatskin is used on gloves and flexible zones.

Do you work with Cordura and mesh?

Yes. Textile jackets and summer gloves use Cordura and mesh with armour pockets as specified.

What is the MOQ for private-label leather motorcycle jackets?

Bulk starts from 50 units per style after a paid sample. Colour and size splits within a style are agreed on the quote.

Can you copy a jacket design I already sell?

We can work from your own sample garment or graded measurements. Send only designs you own or have the right to produce.

Do leather jackets come with CE armour?

Yes, when specified: EN 1621-1 Level 1 or 2 inserts at shoulders and elbows and an EN 1621-2 back insert. A CE rating on an armour insert does not certify the whole garment; garment certification needs test reports for the exact build.
