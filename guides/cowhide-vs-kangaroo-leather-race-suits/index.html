Cowhide vs Kangaroo Leather for Race Suits

Kangaroo leather is lighter and more supple than cowhide while offering similar abrasion resistance, because its fibres run in a more uniform direction. Cowhide in 1.2–1.4 mm is tougher-feeling, more widely available and costs less. Most brands use cowhide, with kangaroo for premium race suits or selected panels.

Hybrid builds

A common premium approach is a cowhide suit with kangaroo in high-movement areas such as the inner arms and around the shoulders. This lowers weight and improves movement while keeping cost below a full kangaroo suit.

How to decide

  • Selling to track-day riders and clubs on value: cowhide.
  • Positioning a flagship race suit: kangaroo or a hybrid.
  • Not sure: order two samples, one of each, and compare weight and feel.

FAQ

Is kangaroo leather stronger than cowhide?

For its thickness, yes. Kangaroo can match cowhide abrasion resistance in a thinner, lighter hide, which is why it is used in premium race suits.

Which leather do most race suits use?

Cowhide, typically 1.2–1.4 mm. It is durable, widely available and costs less than kangaroo.
